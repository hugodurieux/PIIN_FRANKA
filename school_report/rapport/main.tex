\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Abstract.} Torque-level manipulator control requires a faithful dynamics
model, which the analytical rigid-body model derived from a URDF does not
provide once friction and transmission compliance are accounted for. No
published work yet composes an analytical term with a learned residual
simultaneously across seven axes, under hard physical constraints,
payload-conditioned, and queried at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}. We build an automated
pipeline taking the URDF file as sole input and producing a grey-box model
$\taupred = \taurbd + \taures$, whose analytical term is evaluated by RNEA with
no manual derivation and whose payload-conditioned neural residual exposes
three increasing degrees of guarantee (unconstrained, imposed by an augmented
Lagrangian, structurally guaranteed by construction). The model feeds a
\SI{1000}{\hertz} computed-torque-plus-PD law whose gains derive from an error bound
measured on a held-out test set. Over \SI{14830}{} held-out samples the grey-box
model reaches \SIrange{0.29}{1.02}{\newton\meter} per joint
(\SIrange{0.64}{3.09}{\percent} of the torque limit) against
\SI{1.46}{\newton\meter} mean for the analytical model alone, with zero
torque-limit violations where the analytical model violates them on
\SI{0.99}{\percent} of samples. Two results run against the starting hypothesis
and are reported as such: a black box of identical capacity and budget does
\emph{better} than the grey box (\SI{0.45}{\newton\meter} against
\SI{0.62}{\newton\meter} mean), and the learned residual \emph{degrades} the
joints the analytical term already models well --- by a factor of \num{6.9} on
joint~7. The same phenomenon appears in closed loop: disabling the residual
divides joint~5's steady-state bias by \num{22} and halves the Cartesian flange
error.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{A model that is exact and nevertheless wrong}
\label{sec-intro-motivation}

This work was carried out as part of a Master's research internship at the
University of Melbourne, within the Human-Computer-Interaction team,
one of whose research directions is human--agent interaction. A robot that
shares a workspace with a person, or that responds to a high-level instruction
rather than to a programmed trajectory, can only be safe and reactive if its
internal dynamic model is both accurate and queryable in real time. It is this
double requirement  model accuracy, availability inside the control loop 
that motivates choosing a physically instrumentable manipulator such as the
Franka Emika Panda as the target of this work, beyond the interest of the
learning method itself.

Precise control of a manipulator arm relies on knowledge of its dynamics, that
is, of the mapping that relates a desired joint trajectory to the motor torques
that realise it. This relation admits a closed analytical form, recalled in
\cref{sec-prelim}, entirely determined once the geometry and the inertial
parameters of the robot are known and therefore computable directly from a
URDF file (\emph{Unified Robot Description Format}).

The problem is that this analytical form is wrong on a real robot. Dry and
viscous friction in the harmonic drives, transmission compliance, mechanical
backlash, temperature-dependent losses and actuator wear produce a systematic
gap between the torque predicted by the rigid-body model and the torque
actually measured. This gap, called the residual torque here, is of the order
of several newton-metres on a Franka Emika Panda and cannot be modelled
analytically in a reliable way. This is precisely where machine learning comes
in  and it is also where the entire scientific content of the present work
lies: not in replacing the rigid-body model, but in handling what it does not
say.

Two routes oppose each other. The first learns the dynamics end to end, as a
black box: a neural network receives the state and predicts the torque. This
approach is flexible but data-hungry, guarantees no physical property and
generalises poorly outside the training distribution. The second, called
\emph{physics-informed}, constrains the network to respect the structure of the
equations of mechanics  either by writing that structure into the
architecture, or by imposing it through loss terms. Deep Lagrangian
networks~\cite{lutter2019iclr} and, more recently, the work of Liu, Borja and
Della~Santina~\cite{liu2024} have established the theoretical and experimental
viability of this second route on real manipulators.

\subsection{Objective and scientific lock}
\label{sec-verrou}

The objective of this work is to \textbf{build and validate an automatic
pipeline that takes as input the description file of a manipulator alone and
produces as output a robot effectively controlled} by a learned dynamic model,
queried at every period of the control loop. This pipeline comprises the
learning of a grey-box dynamic model, its deployment in a real-time control
law, and a manipulation task serving as an integration test.

Stated this way, the objective may look like engineering. Three distinct
difficulties make it non-trivial, and they are what defines the contribution.

\paragraph{Generality is obtained at the expense of accuracy.}
The literature obtains its best results by deriving the equations of one
particular robot by hand, then fitting a network on top of that structure.
Making the procedure automatic from a plain URDF means giving up any manual
derivation: the kinematic graph, the network dimensions, the excitation of the
training trajectories and the physical bounds must all be extracted from the
description file.

\paragraph{Physical guarantees survive learning badly.}
A freely learned residual can inject energy into the system  physically
absurd for a term meant to represent friction  and predict torques above the
actuator limits. On a 7-degree-of-freedom system these constraints must be
satisfied simultaneously on seven joints whose torque limits differ by a factor
of~7 (\SI{87}{\newton\meter} for the first four joints, \SI{12}{\newton\meter}
for the last three), which naturally unbalances the optimisation.

\paragraph{Offline accuracy does not make a controller.}
A low prediction error on a test set says nothing about closed-loop stability.
Inserting a neural network into a \SI{1000}{\hertz} loop imposes sub-millisecond
inference, reliable real-time scheduling, and a control law whose error can be
bounded.

\subsection{Contributions and state of progress}
\label{sec-contributions}

This work positions itself explicitly with respect to the state of the art
defined by Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{liu2024}, the closest reference: the same
robot (Franka Panda 7~DoF), the same class of methods (physics-informed
networks), the same purpose (model, then control). Four contributions are
claimed.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{An automated ``URDF to model'' pipeline.} The URDF file is the
    sole input: the kinematic graph and the inertial parameters are extracted
    from it by Pinocchio, and the analytical term is evaluated by RNEA with no
    manual derivation whatsoever.
    \item \textbf{The integration of the learned model into a
    \SI{1000}{\hertz} control loop.} The model is queried at every control
    period by a ROS2 node that drives a Franka simulated under MuJoCo.
    \item \textbf{The scaling up to 7~DoF under hard physical constraints.} The
    torque-limit and dissipativity constraints are imposed simultaneously by an
    augmented Lagrangian with dual ascent, and completed by a structural
    guarantee of dissipativity.
    \item \textbf{A measurement of the cost of cross-simulator transfer.} A
    controlled ablation of the residual in closed loop
    (\cref{sec-res-ablation}) quantifies what it costs to deploy, under MuJoCo,
    a residual learned on MuJoCo. It provides the baseline that the
    frozen-backbone fine-tuning protocol  implemented but not yet validated
     has to beat.
\end{enumerate}

The actual state of progress must be stated up front: contributions~1 and~3 are
implemented and quantitatively validated; contribution~2 is validated in closed
loop, but the inference latency has not been formally measured on a dedicated
bench; contribution~4 is measured, but its corollary  does fine-tuning
actually reduce the gap?  remains to be validated (\cref{sec-limites}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec-etat-art}
% =====================================================================

The axis of reading adopted here is the one that genuinely separates the
methods from one another: \textbf{where the physical knowledge is injected into
the learning problem, and what it guarantees in return}. The four families
below are ordered from the strongest constraint to the weakest.

\paragraph{Parameter identification.}
Keep the analytical structure~\eqref{eq-rbd} intact and seek only to identify
its parameters. Sutanto \emph{et al.}~\cite{sutanto2020} propose the modern,
differentiable version of this. The benefit is interpretability; the limitation
is structural: a rigid model can represent neither nonlinear friction nor
compliance, whatever the quality of the identification.

\paragraph{Physics written into the architecture.}
Move the physics into the architecture of the network. Deep Lagrangian
networks~\cite{lutter2019iclr} learn the inertia matrix in Cholesky-factorised
form, which guarantees its positive-definiteness by construction. Duong
\emph{et al.}~\cite{duong2024} transpose the idea into the port-Hamiltonian
formalism on Lie groups. The guarantee then does not depend on the data, but
the price paid is the loss of automatic generality: quantities that the URDF
already provides exactly (inertia, gravity) have to be relearned. Moreover,
although the port-Hamiltonian formalism intrinsically guarantees total
passivity, it turns out to be disproportionate when the only targeted property
is the non-injection of energy by the dissipative terms.

\paragraph{Grey-box models: composing analytical and residual.}
Compose a known analytical term with a learned residual. Djeumou
\emph{et al.}~\cite{djeumou2022} give the reference formulation. Deng
\emph{et al.}~\cite{deng2024} usefully warn that a model can display an
excellent mean error while producing absurd maximum errors --- a direct
argument in favour of hard constraints rather than a plain least-squares
minimisation. The architecture developed in \cref{sec-methode} is an instance
of this grey family.

\paragraph{Constraints specific to the closed loop.}
The closed loop imposes its own requirements, often absent from purely offline
work. The control rate disqualifies heavy integrations such as extended Kalman
filtering~\cite{wang2024cac} at \SI{1000}{\hertz}. Hu \emph{et
al.}~\cite{hu2026} establish a fact that is crucial for payload conditioning:
the backlash error depends on position and velocity, but not on the carried
mass --- which justifies conditioning only the residual term on $\delta$,
rather than the whole model.

\paragraph{The lock addressed here.}
The grey composition is established. It has not yet been carried
\emph{simultaneously} to seven axes, under hard physical constraints,
payload-conditioned, and queried at \SI{1000}{\hertz} inside a real control
loop. It is the conjunction of these four requirements, and not each of them
taken in isolation, that defines the contribution of the present work.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec-prelim}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Notation.}
$\Rset^{n}$ denotes the $n$-dimensional Euclidean space, with $n = 7$ for the
Franka Emika Panda considered throughout this document. The vectors
$\q, \qd, \qdd \in \Rset^{n}$ denote joint positions, velocities and
accelerations respectively. The scalar $\delta \in \Rset_{+}$ denotes the mass
carried at the end effector (the payload). The vector
$\bar{\tau} \in \Rset^{n}_{+}$ collects the per-joint torque limits, and
$\bar{\tau}_{j}$ denotes its $j$-th component. For a vector $v$, $|v|$ is
understood component-wise.

\paragraph{Rigid-body dynamic model.}
For a rigid serial robot with $n$ degrees of freedom, the inverse dynamic model
takes the classical form
%
\begin{equation}
    \tau = M(\q)\,\qdd + C(\q,\qd)\,\qd + G(\q) ,
    \label{eq-rbd}
\end{equation}
%
where $M(\q) \in \Rset^{n \times n}$ is the inertia matrix (symmetric positive
definite), $C(\q,\qd) \in \Rset^{n \times n}$ the matrix of Coriolis and
centrifugal terms, $G(\q) \in \Rset^{n}$ the gravity vector and
$\tau \in \Rset^{n}$ the vector of joint torques.
\Cref{eq-rbd} is entirely determined once the geometry and the inertial
parameters of the robot are known; it is computed efficiently by the recursive
Newton--Euler algorithm (RNEA) from a kinematic and inertial description,
typically a URDF file. Throughout this work the evaluation of \cref{eq-rbd} is
denoted $\taurbd$ and delegated to Pinocchio; it is \emph{never} learned nor
modified.

\paragraph{Franka Emika Panda constants.}
The robot considered has $n = 7$ revolute joints. The torque limits are
$\bar{\tau} = (87, 87, 87, 87, 12, 12, 12)\,\si{\newton\meter}$ and the maximum
velocities $(2.175,\ 2.175,\ 2.175,\ 2.175,\ 2.610,\ 2.610,\
2.610)\,\si{\radian\per\second}$. The factor of~7 between the limits of the
load-bearing joints and those of the wrist is decisive for the balance of the
optimisation, and is the subject of \cref{sec-res-desequilibre}. The target
control rate is \SI{1000}{\hertz}, and the payloads evaluated are
$\delta \in \{0, 1, 3\}\,\si{\kilogram}$.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec-probleme}
% =====================================================================

This section defines formally what is minimised, before \cref{sec-methode} says
how.

\paragraph{Data.}
Let a dataset
%
\begin{equation}
    \Dset = \bigl\{ (\q^{(i)},\ \qd^{(i)},\ \qdd^{(i)},\ \delta^{(i)},\
    \taureal^{(i)}) \bigr\}_{i=1}^{N} ,
    \label{eq-dataset}
\end{equation}
%
be collected on a high-fidelity simulator (MuJoCo), where $\taureal^{(i)}$
is the torque actually applied at sample $i$. The set is split into
$80\,\%$ training, $10\,\%$ validation (early stopping) and $10\,\%$
independent test, the latter serving exclusively to compute the reported
performance and the error bounds $\epsilon_j$.

\paragraph{Model sought.}
We seek a parameterised mapping $\taures(\cdot\,;\theta)$, with parameters
$\theta$, such that the predicted torque
%
\begin{equation}
    \taupred(\q,\qd,\qdd,\delta\,;\theta)
    = \taurbd(\q,\qd,\qdd,\delta)
    + \taures(\q,\qd,\delta\,;\theta)
    \label{eq-modele}
\end{equation}
%
approaches $\taureal$. The term $\taurbd$ is fixed; learning acts on $\theta$
only. Note that $\taures$ does not depend on $\qdd$: that acceleration is
available offline only, and excluding it from the residual's input is what
makes the model queryable inside a \SI{1000}{\hertz} loop that measures $\q$
and $\qd$ alone.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The problem solved is the constrained program
%
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\theta}\ \
    \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \bigl\| \taupred^{(i)}(\theta) - \taureal^{(i)} \bigr\|_{2}^{2}}
    _{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\theta)}
    \quad \text{subject to} \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \bigl|\taupred^{(i)}(\theta)\bigr| \le \bar{\tau}, \\[2pt]
        \taures^{(i)}(\theta)^{\top} \qd^{(i)} \le 0 ,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq-probleme}
\end{equation}
%
for every $i$. The first constraint forbids predicting a torque the actuators
cannot deliver. The second requires the residual to be \emph{dissipative}: it
must never inject mechanical energy into the system, which is the formal
translation of ``this term represents friction''. \Cref{sec-methode} presents
three treatments of these constraints, of increasing rigour.

does not cover:
%
\begin{equation}

    \;+\;
    \underbrace{\taures(\q,\qd,\delta)}_{\text{learned, constrained}} ,

\end{equation}
%
where $\delta \in \Rset_{+}$ denotes the carried mass. \Cref{fig-pipeline}
gives the overall view of the pipeline, making explicit the loss terms and the
gradient flows that connect them to the two trained networks.

\subsection{The learned residual and its three degrees of guarantee}
\label{sec-residu}

\subsubsection{Degree~0: a free, smooth, conditioned residual}
\label{sec-degre0}

The input of the network is the vector
%
\begin{equation}
    x = \bigl[\sin(\q),\ \cos(\q),\ \qd,\ \delta\bigr] \in \Rset^{22}.
    \label{eq-encodage}
\end{equation}
%
Including $\sin(\q)$ and $\cos(\q)$ rather than $\q$ itself serves two purposes:
removing the discontinuity of $\q$ at the angular bounds, and making it
possible to model the torque ripple specific to the Franka's harmonic drives,
whose period is a function of the angle. Feeding $\delta$ directly to the
network does, however, introduce a risk: the model could use it as a bias to
compensate for possible mass errors in the analytical Pinocchio model, which
must be monitored.

The activation chosen is Mish. ReLU is rejected in order to guarantee a
continuous derivative (motor safety: a discontinuous derivative translates into
a torque jerk). Mish offers self-regularisation in the neighbourhood of zero
without the saturation problems of Tanh.

\subsubsection{Degree~1: guarantees imposed by the objective function}
\label{sec-degre1}

The constraints of \cref{eq-probleme} are relaxed into penalties through an
augmented Lagrangian with dual ascent:
%
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
    + \underbrace{\overline{\lambda_{t}\,\psi_{\mathrm{torque}}
      + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,\psi_{\mathrm{torque}}^{2}}
    + \overline{\lambda_{d}\,\psi_{\mathrm{dis}}
      + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,\psi_{\mathrm{dis}}^{2}}}
      _{\text{augmented Lagrangian}} ,
    \label{eq-Ltotal}
\end{equation}
%
with $\psi_{\mathrm{torque}} = \max(0, |\taupred| - \bar{\tau})$ and
$\psi_{\mathrm{dis}} = \max(0, \taures^{\top}\qd)$. The parameter $\rho$ is set
to~1. Unlike a plain quadratic penalty, the multipliers $\lambda_{t},
\lambda_{d}$ are updated by dual ascent, which makes it possible to reach
constraint satisfaction without driving $\rho$ to infinity --- and therefore
without degrading the conditioning of the optimisation.

\subsubsection{Degree~2: a guarantee obtained by construction}
\label{sec-frictionnet}

The friction module produces a diagonal matrix
%
\begin{equation}
    D(x) = \mathrm{diag}\bigl(\mathrm{Softplus}(d) + \varepsilon\bigr),
    \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-6},
    \label{eq-Dmat}
\end{equation}
%
\begin{equation}
    \taufric = -D(x)\,\qd ,
    \label{eq-taufric}
\end{equation}
%
guaranteeing $\taufric^{\top}\qd = -\qd^{\top} D(x)\,\qd \le 0$ for every
$\qd$, since $D(x)$ is positive definite by construction. The guarantee
therefore depends neither on the data nor on the convergence of the
optimisation. Although mathematically robust thanks to $\varepsilon$,
physically, if the network learns a strongly negative weight tied to a large
payload $\delta$, the friction could artificially tend to zero --- the
guarantee bears on the sign, not on the magnitude.

\subsection{Identification and data hygiene}
\label{sec-donnees}

\paragraph{Rigorous separation.} The dataset $\Dset$ is split as indicated in
\cref{sec-probleme}: $80\,\%$ training, $10\,\%$ validation (for early
stopping) and $10\,\%$ independent test, used to report the final performance
and to compute the gains.

\paragraph{Saturation filtering.} Samples clipped by the simulator are removed:
%
\begin{equation}
    \bigl|\tau_{\mathrm{real},j}\bigr| \le \num{0.97}\,\bar{\tau}_j .
    \label{eq-satfilter}
\end{equation}
%
A saturated sample carries no information about the dynamics: it informs about
the actuator bound, not about the torque the motion required. Keeping it
amounts to training the residual to reproduce a clipping.

\subsection{Closing the loop}
\label{sec-boucle}

The control law is a computed torque augmented with a PD term:
%
\begin{equation}
    \taucmd = \taurbd(\q,\qd,\qdd_{\mathrm{d}},\delta) + \taures(\q,\qd,\delta)
    + K_p\,e + K_d\,\dot{e} ,
    \label{eq-ctpd}
\end{equation}
%
where $e = \q_{\mathrm{d}} - \q$ is the tracking error. The gains follow the
stability template of Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{liu2024}:
%
\begin{equation}
    K_{d,jj} = \kappa\,\epsilon_j , \qquad K_{p,jj} = \frac{K_{d,jj}^{2}}{4} .
    \label{eq-gains}
\end{equation}
%
The bound $\epsilon_j$ is the \SI{99.9}{\percent} quantile of the error
evaluated on the test set. This choice mathematically weakens the strict
Lyapunov proof (which requires the absolute worst case), turning stability into
a statistical expectation covered, as a last resort, by the hardware limits.

\paragraph{A counter-intuitive consequence of the template.} Since $K_p$ derives
from $\epsilon_j$, a \emph{better} modelled joint receives a lower gain. Yet the
steady-state error under a constant residual torque is $e_{\mathrm{ss}} =
\tau/K_p$. The best-learned joints therefore track \emph{worse}. This mechanism
is verified experimentally in \cref{sec-res-suivi} and discussed again in
\cref{sec-limites}: it is a limitation of the template of~\cite{liu2024}, not
an implementation defect.

\paragraph{Domain gap (\emph{sim-to-sim gap}).} The data come from mujoco
and the real-time execution also takes place under MuJoCo. This choice, motivated by
software constraints (removal of competing servo loops), introduces an accepted
domain gap, whose cost \cref{sec-res-ablation} measures precisely.

\section{Validation and results}
\label{sec-resultats}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Synthesis.} This section establishes five results, all measured on
held-out data.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The pipeline works end to end.} Starting from the URDF file
    alone, the learned model effectively drives a simulated Franka at
    \SI{1000}{\hertz}, and the grasping sequence completes
    (\cref{sec-res-suivi,sec-limites-integration}).
    \item \textbf{The grey-box model divides the error of the analytical model
    by \num{2.3}}, from \SI{1.46}{} to \SI{0.62}{\newton\meter} on average, on
    the partition used here (\cref{tab-baselines}; scope stated precisely in
    \cref{sec-res-partition}).
    \item \textbf{The physical constraints hold.} Zero torque-limit violation
    over the \SI{14830}{} test samples, where the analytical model alone
    produces them on \SI{0.99}{\percent} of them (\cref{sec-res-garanties}).
    \item \textbf{On these data, a black box of identical capacity is more
    accurate} (\SI{0.45}{} against \SI{0.62}{\newton\meter}) --- but since the
    partition is drawn at the sample level over time series, this gap
    mechanically favours the model that memorises most, and constitutes an
    upper bound rather than an established result
    (\cref{sec-baselines,sec-res-partition}).
    \item \textbf{The learned residual degrades the joints that the analytical
    term already models well}, by a factor of \num{6.9} on joint~7. The same
    effect is measured independently in closed loop, where disabling the
    residual divides joint~5's bias by \num{22}
    (\cref{tab-perjoint-baselines,sec-res-ablation}).
\end{enumerate}

Results~4 and~5 run against the starting hypothesis. They are reported as
results and not as failures: the second identifies a precise and reproducible
failure mode of grey residuals, corroborated by two independent measurements
--- one offline and one in closed loop --- and it points directly at the
correction to be made (\cref{sec-limites}).

\paragraph{Measurement protocol.} Unless stated otherwise, every figure in this
section is measured on the \textbf{held-out test set} (\SI{14830}{} samples),
seen neither by training nor by checkpoint selection. The 80/10/10 partition of
\cref{sec-probleme} is shared by training, by the evaluation of the baselines
and by the computation of $\epsilon_j$, so that every row of
\cref{tab-baselines} bears on exactly the same samples. The comparisons hold
capacity, architecture and epoch budget constant; only the element under study
varies.

This precaution is not rhetorical. Until 31~July 2026 the repository contained
no test set at all: the published RMSE was the validation one, on which the
checkpoint was also selected, and $\epsilon_j$ --- hence the deployed gains ---
was derived from it. The figures below are the first in the project to be
measured outside the selection criterion, and they differ from the previous
ones. They are not, however, out-of-sample in the strong sense: the partition
is drawn at the sample level over time series at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}, which
leaves a dependence between training and test. \Cref{sec-res-partition}
establishes the exact scope of this limitation and what it distorts; it must be
read before any interpretation of the gaps between models.

Training was carried out on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX~3090
(\SI{24}{\gibi\byte}) and an Intel Core i9-12900KF processor (16~cores,
24~threads), under PyTorch~2.13 with CUDA~13.2, with a wall-clock time of
\SI{245}{\second} for \num{200} epochs over
\SI{118644}{} training samples (\SI{209}{\second} for the black-box baseline,
\SI{242}{\second} for the encoding ablation).

\subsection{Baselines and comparison protocol}
\label{sec-baselines}

Three models are compared on the \emph{same} independent test set, with the
same data-splitting protocol and the same stopping criterion.

\paragraph{Analytical baseline (RNEA alone).}
$\taupred = \taurbd$, with no learned term ($\theta$ empty). No
hyperparameters. This baseline measures exactly what the URDF file provides on
its own, and therefore the magnitude of the residual torque that the grey-box
model is tasked with capturing.

\paragraph{Black-box baseline (direct MLP).}
$\taupred = f(x\,;\theta)$ with $x$ given by \cref{eq-encodage}, that is, the
same input and the same capacity as GreyBoxNet, but \emph{without} the

residual. Same architecture (multilayer perceptron, Mish activation), same
number of layers and units, same optimiser and same epoch budget as the
proposed model, so that the measured gap is attributable to the grey structure
and not to capacity.

\paragraph{Proposed model.}
$\taupred = \taurbd + \taures$ following \cref{eq-decompo}, with the three
degrees of guarantee of \cref{sec-residu}.

(\cref{sec-res-desequilibre}) gives it no reason to stay at zero where it
should be.

\subsection{A caveat on the partition, and on what it distorts}
\label{sec-res-partition}

The preceding comparison calls for a methodological caveat that limits its
scope, and it must be stated before drawing anything from it.

The data consist of \num{30} continuous \SI{5}{\second} trajectories ---
\num{10} Fourier excitation segments around Sobol centres, for each of the
three payloads --- sampled at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}. The partition of
\cref{sec-probleme}, however, is \emph{drawn uniformly at the sample level}.
Two consecutive time steps, \SI{1}{\milli\second} apart on the same trajectory,
are therefore assigned independently: every test sample has, in the training
set, near-identical neighbours in $(\q, \qd)$.

\paragraph{What this invalidates, and what it does not.} The \emph{selection}
defect described in \cref{sec-probleme} is indeed corrected: the checkpoint is
no longer chosen on the reported set, and $\epsilon_j$ is no longer derived
from it. That remains the most important correction in the project. The
\emph{independence} of the samples, however, is not established, and
\cref{sec-resultats} must not be read as a measure of generalisation to new
trajectories. It measures interpolation within a family of \num{30} excitation
trajectories.

\paragraph{The effect is not the same for the three models, and that is the
point.} A neighbour \SI{1}{\milli\second} away brings nothing to the analytical
model, which is fitted to no data: the RNEA row of \cref{tab-baselines} is
insensitive to this leakage. The two learned models benefit from it, but
unequally, and the one favoured most is the one that has to memorise most. The
grey-box model has only a residual to learn, inertia and gravity being supplied
to it analytically; the black box has to reconstruct everything from the data
alone.

A quantitative clue confirms this, and it is hard to explain otherwise. The
black box does \emph{not} receive the acceleration $\qdd$ as input: its
encoding is that of \cref{eq-encodage}, namely $(\q, \qd, \delta)$. It is
therefore structurally incapable of representing the inertial torque
$M(\q)\,\qdd$, the dominant term of the dynamics --- and it nevertheless wins
by \SI{28}{\percent}. With only \num{30} trajectories and a sample-level draw,
the pair $(\q, \qd)$ almost always identifies the trajectory and the phase,
which makes $\qdd$ implicitly recoverable by memorisation. The measured gap is
therefore at least partly an artefact of the partition, and it bears precisely
on the comparison this work set out to establish.

\paragraph{The correction, and its status.} The correct measurement is a
\emph{group-wise} partition: distributing the \num{30} segments --- and not the
\num{148304} samples --- between training, validation and test, so that no test
trajectory has ever been seen. The black box then loses the support of
memorisation and has to extrapolate, which the analytical term of the grey-box
model does by construction. An implementation of this group-wise partition
exists in the repository and a preliminary measurement has been carried out,
but it rests on too few test trajectories to be reported here: its analysis is
left to future work. Consequently, the \SI{28}{\percent} gap of
\cref{tab-baselines} must be read as an upper bound on the advantage of the
black box rather than as an established result, and the comparisons of this
section as measurements of interpolation within the excitation family used.

\paragraph{The spatial encoding weighs almost nothing.} The
$[\sin(\q), \cos(\q)]$ encoding of \cref{eq-encodage} is justified in

and making it possible to represent torque ripple. Measured, it contributes

evidently not a dominant term in these data.

\paragraph{FrictionNet does not bring accuracy.} The dissipative module
(\cref{sec-frictionnet}, novelty N2) costs \SI{1.4}{\percent} of RMSE when
removed. Its value is therefore not accuracy but the \emph{guarantee}: it makes
$\taufric^{\top}\qd \le 0$ true by construction rather than by penalty. That is
a property the black box, more accurate, does not offer. The claim must be
formulated in those terms and not in terms of error.

\subsection{Compliance with the physical guarantees}
\label{sec-res-garanties}

The violation of the torque-limit constraint is \emph{zero} over the whole test
set. The residual dissipativity violation falls to \num{6e-4} on the training
data, a non-zero value because degree~1 (\cref{sec-degre1}) relaxes the
constraint into a penalty; the component covered by degree~2
(\cref{sec-frictionnet}) is, for its part, exactly zero by construction.

\subsection{Closed-loop trajectory tracking}
\label{sec-res-suivi}

The learned model does drive the robot: law~\eqref{eq-ctpd} is evaluated at
\SI{1000}{\hertz} by a ROS2 node and the simulated Franka follows the planned
trajectories. The observed behaviour quantitatively confirms the
counter-intuitive consequence announced in \cref{sec-boucle}: joint~5, one of
the best modelled and therefore one of the most weakly gained
($K_{p,55} = \num{4.41}$, against \num{48.20} for joint~2), carries a
systematic steady-state bias, always of the same sign, of the order of
\SIrange{0.013}{0.053}{\radian} depending on the configuration. This bias grows
with the arm's reach (about \SI{-0.044}{\radian} at \SI{0.45}{\meter},
\SI{-0.050}{\radian} at \SI{0.65}{\meter}, \SI{-0.066}{\radian} at
\SI{0.70}{\meter}), the signature of a load-dependent term rather than of a
constant offset --- that is, exactly $e_{\mathrm{ss}} = \tau/K_p$.

\subsection{Ablation of the learned residual under cross-simulator transfer}
\label{sec-res-ablation}

residual in closed loop. Under a strictly identical configuration (object at

residual is enabled and then disabled, everything else being held constant.

\paragraph{What this result establishes, and what it does not.}
The checkpoint evaluated was trained on Mujoco data and also deployed under
MuJoCo. It has therefore learned the gap between RNEA and \emph{Mujoco's}
dynamics, and adds it to a MuJoCo model that does not exhibit that gap: it is
being evaluated out of distribution. The defensible conclusion is that
\textbf{the residual learned on Mujoco degrades tracking under cross-simulator
transfer}. This is \emph{not} a finding that the grey-box approach fails, its
offline accuracy being established in \cref{sec-res-stage1}. What this result
does provide is the quantified baseline that the frozen-backbone fine-tuning
protocol (\cref{sec-limites}) has to beat, and it is on that account a useful
result rather than a failure.

One potential confounder must be ruled out: does the ablation also change the
gains? No --- the gain margin is explicitly held constant between the two
conditions. Moreover, the effect of a lower gain would go the \emph{opposite}
way (it would increase $e_{\mathrm{ss}} = \tau/K_p$), so the result survives
that confounder.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec-discussion}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The uncorrected optimisation bias}
\label{sec-res-desequilibre}
The MSE loss is unweighted, which encourages the network to ignore the wrist
(limit \SI{12}{\newton\meter}) in favour of the load-bearing joints
(\SI{87}{\newton\meter}). This is directly visible in \cref{tab-perjoint}:
relative to the torque limit, the error of joints~5 to~7
(\SIrange{2.40}{3.09}{\percent}) is nearly three times that of joints~1 to~4
(\SIrange{0.64}{1.17}{\percent}), although it looks better in absolute value.
This defect justifies the future implementation of a diagonal weighting
$\mathrm{diag}(1/\bar{\tau}_j^2)$ in the loss.

\subsection{Limitations of the integration with the physics engine}
\label{sec-limites-integration}

The grasping task served as an integration test, and not as a measure of the
quality of the model: it is reported here, in the discussion, rather than in
the results, because what it measured turned out to be the quality of the
middleware and not that of the learned dynamics.

\paragraph{A configuration fault that imitated a physics fault.}
For a week, three joints appeared to refuse any motion. The final diagnosis has
nothing to do with the learned model nor with the control law: the
\code{ResetWorld} service of \code{mujoco_ros2_control} silently restores the
plugin's internal position PID, without going through the mode-switch routine.
No layer reports it --- the controller manager continues to announce the effort
controller as active, commands continue to be published, and the arm holds
itself up convincingly. Every published torque was nevertheless discarded, the
arm settling at
$\q_{\mathrm{home}} - \tau_{\mathrm{gravity}}/p_{\mathrm{PID}}$, a value
reproducible to within \SI{1e-5}{\radian} across three independent runs. It is
this arithmetic signature, and not an error message, that made it possible to
identify the cause.

\paragraph{A transferable methodological lesson.} A check that cannot fail is
worse than no check at all. The available checks all queried the layer that
\emph{lied} (the controller manager) rather than the one that \emph{acts} (the
plugin itself). The diagnosis only progressed once verification was made
against the plugin's own log.

\paragraph{State after correction.} Once this defect was corrected, the
complete grasping sequence succeeds: the object is grasped and lifted. On the
reference configuration (object at \SI{0.55}{\meter}), three runs out of three
succeed, with a final grasp error between \SI{13.9}{\milli\meter} and
\SI{14.9}{\milli\meter} for a tolerance of \SI{20}{\milli\meter}, i.e. a spread
of \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter}. At \SI{0.45}{\meter} the rate falls to two out of
three, and at \SI{0.70}{\meter} the sequence only completes at the cost of long
null-space reconfiguration detours, with convergence margins of a few tenths of
a millimetre. The edge of the reachable envelope therefore manifests itself as
a gradient, not as a sharp boundary.

\paragraph{What this task does not validate.} The configuration sweep remains
partial: only distance and, partially, lateral offset have been explored. The
mass of the object, the height of the support surface and the grasp orientation
have not been tested. In particular, payload conditioning through $\delta$ ---
that is, the most specific scientific contribution of this work --- has never
been exercised in a real grasp, for lack of having varied the mass of the
grasped object.

\subsection{Structural and methodological limitations}
\label{sec-limites}
\begin{description}
    \item[Partition drawn at the sample level.] The data are \num{30}
    continuous trajectories sampled at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}, but the partition
    is drawn per sample: every test point has neighbours
    \SI{1}{\milli\second} away in the training set
    (\cref{sec-res-partition}). The selection bias is corrected, sample
    independence is not. The correction --- a segment-wise partition --- has
    not been executed, and it conditions the interpretation of all the
    comparisons between learned models in \cref{tab-baselines}. This is the
    most serious limitation of this work.
    \item[The grey structure is not justified by accuracy on these data.]
    Measured under an identical protocol, a black box of the same capacity and
    the same budget does \SI{28}{\percent} better (\cref{tab-baselines}) ---
    subject to the unresolved caveat of the previous point. The justification
    of the grey approach must therefore rest on what the black box does not
    offer --- structural guarantees, automatic extraction from the URDF, no
    relearning of inertia and gravity --- and not on the prediction error. The
    present document no longer claims superiority in accuracy over the black
    box.
    \item[The residual is not regularised per joint.] It degrades the joints
    that RNEA already models well, by a factor of \num{6.9} on joint~7
    (\cref{tab-perjoint-baselines}), and the same effect is found in closed
    loop (\cref{sec-res-ablation}). No mechanism encourages the residual to
    stay at zero where the analytical term is already correct. The
    $\mathrm{diag}(1/\bar{\tau}_j^2)$ weighting of
    \cref{sec-res-desequilibre} is a partial correction; an explicit per-joint
    penalty on $\|\taures\|$, calibrated on RNEA's residual error, would be a
    more direct one.
    \item[Heavy distribution tail, untreated.] The maximum error reaches
    \SI{81.8}{\newton\meter} on joint~2 for a $p_{99.9}$ of
    \SI{6.94}{\newton\meter}. Inspection of the worst samples shows real
    torques close to the limit associated with near-zero analytical
    predictions, which suggests collection artefacts (contacts, transients)
    rather than model defects. These samples are neither filtered nor analysed,
    and they drive $\epsilon_j$ directly, hence the deployed gains.
    \item[Payload conditioning not exercised in closed loop.] The model is
    trained at $\delta \in \{0,1,3\}\,\si{\kilogram}$, but the update of
    $\delta$ during a real grasp has never been triggered
    (\cref{sec-limites-integration}).
    \item[Sim-to-sim fine-tuning implemented, not validated.] The
    frozen-backbone protocol exists but has not been run. \Cref{tab-ablation}
    provides it with the baseline to beat; until it is run, contribution~4
    remains a tooling contribution.
    \item[Spatial under-sampling.] Ten Sobol configurations leave uncovered
    regions in $\Rset^7$. Out-of-distribution generalisation is not claimed ---
    and \cref{sec-res-ablation} shows concretely what it costs.
    \item[Statistical stability.] Choosing a quantile for $\epsilon_j$ makes
    closed-loop stability empirical rather than absolute. The template
    of~\cite{liu2024} moreover carries the structural defect identified in
    \cref{sec-boucle}: deriving $K_p$ from an error bound makes the
    best-modelled joints the worst tracked.
    \item[Inference latency not measured.] The loop runs at
    \SI{1000}{\hertz} with no observed overrun, but no dedicated bench has
    measured the distribution of the inference latency.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec-conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This work has built an automated pipeline that starts from the URDF file of a
Franka Emika Panda 7~DoF alone and produces a simulated robot effectively
controlled by a grey-box dynamic model. The analytical term is evaluated by
RNEA with no manual derivation whatsoever, and learning is confined to a
residual conditioned on the carried payload, constrained by an augmented
Lagrangian and, for its dissipative part, structurally guaranteed. On held-out
data the model reaches \SIrange{0.29}{1.02}{\newton\meter} per joint, against
\SI{1.46}{\newton\meter} on average for the analytical model alone, with zero
torque-limit violation where the analytical model produces them on
\SI{0.99}{\percent} of samples; and it is queried at \SI{1000}{\hertz} inside a
computed-torque loop that actually drives the robot.

Two measurements nevertheless contradict the starting hypothesis, and they are
the most instructive ones. A black box of identical capacity and budget is
\SI{28}{\percent} more accurate than the grey-box model, and the learned
residual degrades the joints that the analytical term already models well ---
a factor of \num{6.9} on joint~7. The interest of the grey structure therefore
does not lie in raw accuracy but in what the black box does not offer:
guarantees by construction, and an automatic pipeline that never has to relearn
the inertia and the gravity that the URDF already provides. It must be
formulated in those terms.

The third result is negative at first sight and exploitable thereafter: a
controlled ablation shows that a residual learned on MuJoCo \emph{degrades}
tracking when deployed under MuJoCo, by a factor of \num{22} on joint~5's
steady-state bias. Far from invalidating the grey approach, this finding
quantifies the cost of cross-simulator transfer and provides the baseline that
frozen-backbone fine-tuning has to beat.

\paragraph{Future work.} Three lines of work are priorities, ordered by
decreasing scientific value. \emph{First}, run the frozen-backbone fine-tuning
on MuJoCo data and replay exactly the ablation of \cref{tab-ablation}: this is
the direct validation of contribution~4. \emph{Second}, measure the two missing
baselines (\cref{tab-baselines}) and conduct the spatial-encoding ablation,
without which the contribution of the grey structure remains asserted rather
than demonstrated. \emph{Third}, exercise payload conditioning by varying the
mass of the grasped object and updating $\delta$ at the moment of the grasp,
which would test in closed loop the most specific contribution of this work. In
the longer term, moving to the physical robot remains the milestone that will
give the sim-to-real gap-reduction protocol its meaning.
